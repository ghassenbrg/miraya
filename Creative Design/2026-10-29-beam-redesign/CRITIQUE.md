# Design rationale and revision record

**PROPOSAL ONLY — NOT IMPLEMENTED**

The Beam reference is a finish and identity direction, not a screen template. The redesign keeps its opposing amber/blue planes and connection light, then removes the reference’s Devices/Settings bottom navigation in favor of one native sharing flow. TV UI is rebuilt around distance and focus rather than enlarged Mac rows.

## Revisions made during review

1. **Flat icons failed the quality bar.** The first deterministic tiles were too flat beside the approved reference. Their thin vertical seam resembled a white scaffold, and half-opacity inactive planes became muddy. These were rejected as the final large-icon finish.
2. **Optical artwork was developed explicitly.** Built-in image generation produced a finished sender tile, a narrowly changed receiver companion and a light future-iOS proposal. The selected finish has rich internal light, restrained rim depth and a concentrated white connection. Original sources are preserved. Rounded vector apertures remove peripheral generation specks without destructively altering the source files.
3. **Vectors were refined to match.** Logo/state marks keep deterministic geometry and a short luminous connection rather than copying material pixels or drawing a white H. Large icon material and small optical geometry have separate sources, named clearly. The raster finish is not misrepresented as an all-vector illustration.
4. **Role distinction became subtle.** Warm source versus cool destination emphasis preserves one identity. At 16–32 px, filled versus outlined endpoints provides a shape cue. Neither role relies on a tiny arrow or badge that disappears in a Dock or launcher.
5. **Mac ready initially clipped its final row.** The standard window/body spacing was revised so the complete TV-picture row is visible in ready state. Secondary controls scroll in live state while Pause/Stop remain pinned. Destination duplication was removed from live state so effective output appears earlier.
6. **TV overlay was too tall.** An inactive future mirror-pause placeholder and implementation note occupied the user’s content. Both were removed from the app overlay; the capability boundary remains in review documentation. Four current mirror actions now form a smaller overlay. Direct-media pause remains active and separate.
7. **Simulation controls moved out of the product.** Advancing a first-picture event or simulating Mac confirmation belongs in the review toolbar, not inside a proposed application screen.
8. **Navigation behavior was corrected.** Paused source changes and connection-details dismissal preserve pause. Closing a TV picture panel returns to live controls rather than stopping. D-pad candidates are confined to the active panel/dialog. Individual forget actions update only their named item. Direct HTTPS selections retain media mode.
9. **Contrast was treated as a control property.** Neutral input/button boundaries were strengthened independently of decorative separators. Focus uses shape, fill and outline, and does not depend on a glow. Reduced Motion stops nonessential activity.

10. **The TV background was too plain.** The owner explicitly preferred the original reference’s mountain lake. The receiver now uses matching cinematic artwork with an amber plane/reflection and cool mountain layers. The main copy is centered, pairing gains a glass code panel, and the duplicate right-side Beam decoration is removed. Live examples use labelled streamed wallpaper; real content still takes over the whole surface. Pixel-based contrast testing found four weak text regions on the scenery; brighter supporting text and a dark connection-loss text surface corrected them.

## Assessment

The two planes do not use AirPlay’s screen/triangle or Chromecast’s corner waves. They have no shield/tunnel/VPN metaphor. The abstract silhouette is intentionally simple enough for micro sizes; the warm/cool optical light and endpoint relationship give it personality. This is a design judgment, not trademark clearance or proof of global uniqueness.

Beam appears where it communicates a connection state, not on every button. The Mac has no decorative hero or fake desktop. Technical information is secondary; selected maximum and delivered result remain distinct. TV pairing numbers dominate the composition, and live content has no permanent application chrome. The icon’s central light remains visible at large sizes while an explicit one-pixel seam prevents disappearance at micro size.

The raster icons have more optical depth than the vector construction. That is deliberate, not an excuse to deliver only raster logos: all lockups and wordmarks remain scalable vectors, and material sources/masks remain available for refinement. Small-size and role boards make the tradeoff reviewable.

## Remaining acceptance work after design approval

Review on the actual TV at 2–4 m, including overscan and its panel brightness; review native VoiceOver/TalkBack and macOS increased contrast/text settings after implementation; tune real native transition timing; verify system file/permission sheets and hardware Back/volume events. A static browser review cannot substitute for those checks. No streaming-performance claim is derived from this design prototype.
